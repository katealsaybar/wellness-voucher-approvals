Belle’s reasoning, and it is a design rather than a round number: a short window brings the friend in quickly, and because purchases run only to 30 September, two months from purchase means every gift card in the campaign is dead by 30 November and none of it lands in December. Fourteen characters, typed by hand at a till and sometimes read aloud on the phone. The emirate is dropped because it is derivable, SAA and KCA are Abu Dhabi and AQ and MC are Dubai. The year is dropped because purchases close 30 September 2026. The expiry is a printed field, not part of the code, because Belle needs it editable and a reissued card would otherwise need a new number.

One buyer, one number. Every card in a buyer’s set shares her sequence; only the type letter changes. Season of You at Khalifa City A, the 42nd voucher that branch has sold, is seven cards: WV-SM-KCA-0042, three friend cards WV-SG-KCA-0042-1 to -3, WV-SB-KCA-0042, WV-SR-KCA-0042 and WV-SK-KCA-0042. When a friend walks in with -0042-2, reception reads 0042 and lands on the buyer without searching. Same reasoning as the two-month clocks: one number to hold, not several.

The sequence counts buyers, per branch, not cards. Four branches drawing from one shared counter would need live shared state, and two tills could both write 0043 on the same afternoon with nothing to show an error. Four separate counters make that collision impossible instead of managing it, and a code can be issued with the network down. Each branch starts at 0001. A voided voucher retires its number; gaps are fine, reuse is not.

The branch letters mean issued at, not valid at. The offer is emirate-wide, so KCA on a card does not tie it to Khalifa City A. Saadiyat is the exception to watch: with no working Stripe there, those payments land under KCA, but the serial follows the till the client stood at. A Saadiyat sale is SAA even when the money reads KCA, or every Saadiyat voucher disappears into Khalifa City’s numbers.

Four of the five card types print at the till; this one is issued later, against the same sequence. How the reminders work (client self-exit)

The nurture reminders are written as a kind reminder, never a "pay now" chase. Every email and WhatsApp message carries a client self-exit: an email button ("Already got yours? Tap here and we'll stop the reminders") and a WhatsApp quick-reply button or "DONE" keyword. When the client taps it, the sequence stops immediately and flags the contact centre to confirm the payment. This means we do not rely on the branch to report a sale before the reminders stop, and even if a message reaches someone who already paid at the branch, the soft tone keeps it from causing offence. Three Exits, every conversation

Nobody is ever cornered. Every conversation ends with three ways out, and all three are a good outcome:

  1. Take it for herself.
  2. Hand the gift card that comes with it to someone who would never buy one for herself.
  3. Come back next season. No pressure, no follow-up guilt.

A pressured sale is not a sale we want.
