Reception

You are the only part of this campaign a client actually meets. Everything below is what you say, what you do at the till, and the four things that must never come out of your mouth.

4The rule that breaks three things if you forget it

This is the biggest launch risk on the whole campaign, and it takes two seconds to prevent.

Every in-salon sale

“Tag the tier when you tag the sale.”

When a client pays through a Stripe link, her tier is written onto her record automatically. When she pays at the till or on Tabby, somebody applies voucher:paid by hand and the tier is usually missed. Belle and the coordinators expect most purchases to happen in salon, so this is the normal case, not the rare one.

If the tier is missing, three things fail and none of them shows an error:

  • Her welcome email leaves out the Self-Care Bonus she just paid for.
  • The expiry reminder never fires, because it cannot work out whether she is at 5, 8 or 11 months.
  • On All-In VIP Year, the 8 Pillars Journal never sends.

Nothing looks wrong from our side. The client simply does not get the thing.

Tell her to give the friend cards out early, and tell her why. The clock starts when she buys. If she holds a card for a month and then gifts it, her friend has one month, and she will reasonably assume the friend had two.
You do not work these dates out by hand. Open the till tool, put in the branch, the tier, her name and the date she paid, and it gives you her serial, every card in her set with the right value on it, and all four expiry dates. Then print the card she needs as a PDF. It runs on the four rules in the table above and nothing else, so if the tool and this table ever disagree, the table is what was agreed and the tool is the bug.

It asks you to sign in the first time on each computer. Use the salon email and password, not Kate’s. It stays signed in after that, so it is once per machine and not once per shift. It asks because the tool hands out real card numbers and a number cannot be taken back, so the log has to be able to say who issued what.

7Cancellations, and the thing she is really asking

Her credit is never at risk. Cancelling, rescheduling, arriving late or missing an appointment does not touch it. It stays on her account for the full validity period of her tier. Say that first, because it is the actual worry underneath the question.

Everything else follows the salon’s normal booking, cancellation and no-show policy, unchanged. There is no special campaign rule and there never was. If she asks about notice periods or deposits, quote the standing policy exactly as you would for any other booking.

Do not invent a campaign policy. The voucher itself cannot be cancelled or refunded once bought, and once she buys it, it is in her name. If she wants it to go to someone else she chooses that at the point of purchase, not afterwards.

8The four things never to say

  • “Free.” Nothing here is free. She placed the credit.
  • “Unlimited.” The credit is a spending cap, at every tier.
  • “Your kit is included.” It is an allowance towards the kit, and she always pays a difference.
  • Any colour cap number. There is no cap. If someone quotes AED 700, that was a proposal and it was dropped on 19 August. Colour is a ladder of services, never an amount.
